# Competitive Analysis: Prodigy vs Problocks Light

## Where Prodigy Beats Us

**Free for teachers.** Not $1/student. Zero. No procurement, no budget approval, no IT ticket.

**10+ years of content.** Thousands of hand-crafted, curriculum-aligned questions (grades 1-8). Mapped to state standards (TEKS, Common Core). Our AI-generated questions are untested.

**100M+ student accounts.** Network effects, word-of-mouth, social proof. We have zero.

**Professional art.** Full character animations, hundreds of pets, elaborate worlds, boss battles. Our Phase 1 is colored rectangles with a `?` over NPCs.

**Parent product.** Prodigy Premium ($8/month) — parent reports, rewards, unlocks. Recurring consumer revenue we don't have.

**Already in Texas schools.** Harmony Science Academy teachers may already use it.

---

## Where Prodigy is Weak (Our Openings)

**Math-only (grades 1-8).** No science, economics, English, history. We're multi-subject. If a school wants one platform across all classes, Prodigy can't do that.

**Static question bank.** Students see repeats. Our AI generates fresh questions every time, adapts in real-time to gaps, adjusts difficulty per-student. Better pedagogy.

**Free tier is deliberately limited.** Nags kids to get parents to pay Premium. Students get frustrated hitting paywalls mid-game. Teachers complain constantly. We have zero paywalls — every student gets the full experience.

**Teachers have no real control.** Can assign "focus areas" but can't create custom questions, restrict to specific topics, control play times, or manually adjust difficulty. Our teacher dashboard gives full control.

**Learning is a toll booth.** Students battle monsters; the question is just a gate to continue fighting. Learning is an interruption. Our approach (NPC dialogue, contextual obstacles, story-integrated questions) makes learning feel like part of the world.

**No sponsorship-in-context.** "Texas Instruments is shipping 14,500 calculators..." teaches AND builds brand familiarity. Prodigy's questions are generic. Sponsors get zero value from Prodigy. This is a revenue stream they literally cannot compete on.

**Basic adaptive algorithm.** Adjusts difficulty up/down but doesn't map knowledge gaps at subtopic level or use gap-pushing strategy (60/25/15). Our AI identifies specific weaknesses and pushes on them.

## How We Win Against Prodigy

**Don't compete head-to-head on math games.** We lose that fight today.

### 1. Multi-subject pitch
Pitch to the school, not the math teacher. "One platform for every class." If science, English, and economics teachers all need this — we're not competing with Prodigy, we're filling a gap they can't.

### 2. Teacher control
Target teachers frustrated with Prodigy's lack of customization. "Restrict topics, create custom questions, adjust difficulty per student, control when they play."

### 3. No paywalls
Target schools where parents complained about Premium upsells. "Every student gets the full experience. No kid feels left out."

### 4. Prove better learning outcomes
Run a pilot: 30 students on Prodigy, 30 on Problocks Light, same topic, 4 weeks. Pre/post test. If our adaptive AI produces better results, that data kills Prodigy. Data beats brand.

### 5. Leverage the Harmony connection
Prodigy sells top-down to millions. We sell personally to people who know us. One champion teacher who loves our dashboard is worth more than Prodigy's marketing team. Win one school, get results, expand with data.

## Key Positioning

> "We're not replacing Prodigy for math. We're giving your science teacher, your English teacher, and your economics teacher the same engagement tool — and the math version actually adapts better."

The real risk isn't that Prodigy is better. It's that a teacher says "we already have Prodigy, why do we need another game?" The answer is multi-subject + real teacher control + no paywalls + better adaptive AI.

Prodigy is a **math game company**. We're building an **adaptive learning platform**.
